MIT Media Lab,
and where it meets my work

The MIT Media Lab is a research building where about twenty-four small groups work on things that do not fit in any one department — instruments, materials, satellites, robots, ways of talking to each other. Each group is five to twenty people, usually one professor and their graduate students.

I went through ten of those groups and their neighbours to see what they are currently making. What follows is not a summary of the Media Lab. It identifies specific places where their work and mine share a mechanism, method, question or trajectory. Some are close; others are useful because they diverge.

Everything here is public information from their own websites, gathered in August 2026. This page is for me, and for anyone who wants to understand what I have been doing and what company it keeps. It is not a ranking and not an argument that these are equivalent — only a record of what the comparisons make visible.

Eighteen places where their work and mine meet. Eleven are featured below. The label on each card identifies the strongest form of connection; open “Where they differ” to see what the comparison does not claim.
